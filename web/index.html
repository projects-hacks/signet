Proof of origin for documents

An invoice should be able to prove who sent it.

Signet puts the issuer's own domain behind every document. They publish a public key in DNS. Each document carries a signature over the fields that decide where money goes. Anyone can check it, with no account and nothing installed.

Detection is losing

One question

How it works

Three moves, and none of them involve us

The issuer publishes a key

One TXT record on the domain they already own. Nobody else can put it there, which is the entire trust anchor. No certificate authority, no registry, no account with Signet. 

_signet.northpost.dev  TXT
"v=SIGNET1; k=ed25519;
 p=Kqf0GFh29fslh099Tr9ruRvy6qI7ITeKC5KY8Wt0YWI="

The document carries a signature

Amount, currency, account number, document id, issuer, timestamp. The fields that decide where money goes, signed as one canonical string and printed on the page as a QR code and as text. 

S1|amt=15580.00;bic=NWBKGB2L;
cur=USD;iban=GB29NWBK60161331926819;
id=INV-2026-0611;iss=northpost.dev|T2DLIRT…

The reader checks it

Read the key from DNS, verify the signature over the fields, compare those fields against what the page actually shows. Two of those three need no software at all. 

$ openssl pkeyutl -verify …
Signature Verified Successfully

Six questions, asked separately

Every check reports its own answer, and the verdict is a function of all of them

No model decides anything. decide(signals) is pure and total: the same signals always produce the same verdict, which is why it has a golden test suite rather than a confidence score.

Signature

Does the key at the issuer's domain verify this signature over these exact fields?

Identity

Is that domain the one enrolled for the brand the document claims to be from?

Lookalike

Does the signing domain read as somebody else's, while not being theirs?

Fidelity

Does the page in front of you show what the signature actually covers?

Duplicate

Has this exact document been submitted here before?

Counterparty

What does the live web publish for this brand, and does it agree with who signed?

A check that cannot reach its evidence reports unknown, never pass. Certification needs positive evidence, so an outage lowers a verdict rather than inventing one.

Every one of these is load bearing. Remove it and something the product claims stops being true.

Issuance

Doctavian

Generates the document from a verified record. The template loops over the line items, sums them, and shows a high value warning only when the total crosses the threshold, so the payee block is never typed by hand.

adapters/doctavian.py

The trust anchor

name.com

Publishes the issuer's public key as a TXT record on their own domain, and answers the registrar sweep that finds which permutations of that domain somebody already owns.

adapters/namecom.py, issue/sweep.py

Reading the page

Nutrient

Extracts the payment fields from the document as it was received, with a confidence and a bounding box for each. That is what the fidelity check compares against the signature, and what the reader sees highlighted on the page.

adapters/nutrient.py

Live diligence

SerpApi

Asks the open web which domain a brand actually publishes. This is the only check that can catch a forger impersonating a company that never enrolled with anyone, which is most companies.

adapters/serpapi.py

The human gate

Foxit

PDF Services does the reversible work: assemble the enrolment authorisation, then read it back as text to confirm the authorisation hash survived into the file. eSign puts it in front of a person. The key is published only after the executed document comes back carrying that hash.

adapters/foxit.py

System of record

Xano

Issuers, the submissions ledger that makes duplicate detection real, the evidence cache that keeps the search budget honest, and the append only audit log behind every verdict.

adapters/xano.py, xano/

The agent

NVIDIA NIM

Drives enrolment from a plain sentence. It orchestrates and never decides: every gate is a precondition in code, because a model told to hurry will skip a check it was asked to perform.

docs/adr/0007

Cryptography

Ed25519, over DNS

Signatures are sixty four bytes and keys are thirty two, which is what lets a whole signed payload fit in one QR code and one TXT record. Read from two independent resolvers that have to agree.

core/signing.py, adapters/dns_multi.py

What a certified verdict does not mean

It means the named domain signed these fields and they have not changed since. It does not mean the goods arrived, the work was done, or that the invoice is owed. A real company can certify a real invoice for something you never ordered.

Signet moves the argument from "is this document genuine" to "is this charge correct", which is the argument you can actually have with a counterparty. The rest of the limits are written down rather than left for you to discover.
